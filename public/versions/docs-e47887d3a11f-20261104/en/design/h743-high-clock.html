H743 V-only high-clock profile¶

Use hsi64-pll384-cache-off when a cold STM32H743ZIT6 reference needs distinct CPU, bus and peripheral clock domains. This page explains the current fixed profile, its joint owner and failure boundaries. It covers V revision 0x2003 only, with Cache off. Physical HIL remains not_executed; software execution does not measure supply, junction temperature, clock accuracy or communication.

One assembly selects one clock owner¶

FIG-05 describes the common Platform lifecycle. For this exact reference, its SoC startup expands to clock owner, System timebase and selected consumers, in that order. The reverse path must withdraw consumers and System before the clock owner. The diagram is not a frequency-transition sequence or physical proof; the exact clock stages below specialize its lifecycle responsibilities.

One TOML clock selection resolves through reviewed SoC facts into immutable clock-domain IR. The generated bundle selects the private implementation and CMake selects its translation unit. The typed factory still returns existing static faces without MMIO, allocation, clock setup or discovery. Runtime calls enter the selected provider directly through its shared read-only operations table.

The private clock contract owns admission and clock responsibility. System owns TIM2 and its vector. The generated lifecycle glue orders them and preserves startup and cleanup diagnostics. Their effect masks are different namespaces; the platform projects retained responsibility without treating the numeric masks as interchangeable.

Admit an exact cold configuration¶

Automatic high-profile admission requires DEV_ID 0x450, REV_ID 0x2003 (V) and the factory-default HSI user trim. ES0392 Rev15 Table 2 maps 0x1003 to both Y and W, and 0x2001 to X. The reviewed DS12110 Rev11 electrical chapter explicitly covers V; the Y/W ambiguity and lack of an explicitly reviewed X chapter prevent admitting those IDs. They and unknown revisions return UNSUPPORTED without acquiring effects. There is no automatic fallback or user-overridable revision allowlist.

Startup requires the real reset-owned call sequence before SysTick, a scheduler, I/O or another clock owner has run. Matching register values cannot prove that history. The diagnostic deliberately avoids reading SysTick CTRL because its COUNTFLAG is read-clear; zero LOAD/VAL are not proof that ENABLE is clear. Bootloader adoption and runtime frequency scaling require separate contracts.

Cold checks include ready LDO/VOS3, inactive Flash pulses and programming modes, reset PLL configuration, quiet consumers and known-clean disabled caches. Readable disabled cache bits do not prove that foreign dirty lines are absent. Supply, temperature, package and PCB cannot be qualified by IDCODE or readback.

Where the HSI envelope comes from¶

DS12110 Rev11 Table 134, page 232, gives V-cut factory HSI frequency 63.7..64.3 MHz at VDD 3.3 V and junction 30 °C. It separately specifies voltage drift −0.12..+0.03% over VDD 1.62..3.6 V and temperature drift −2..+1% over −40 °C..TJmax. Factory frequency limits use production testing; the full temperature drift uses characterization. These assurance levels remain distinct. The narrower −20..105 °C row is not the full-temperature envelope. Suffix 6 junction limits must not be confused with ambient limits or thermal dissipation.

Conservative multiplicative endpoints are 63.7 × 0.98 × 0.9988 = 62.3510888 MHz and 64.3 × 1.01 × 1.0003 = 64.9624829 MHz. The additive lower estimate 63.7 − 64.3 × (0.02 + 0.0012) is 62.33684 MHz. The selected 62..65 MHz interval covers these compositions; it is a reviewed engineering envelope, not a literal datasheet interval. RM0433 Rev9’s PLL-ready deviation adds the conservative factor 0.98..1.02, yielding 62 × 6 × 0.98 = 364.56 MHz through 65 × 6 × 1.02 = 397.8 MHz. The design stays below the reviewed VOS1 CPU/HCLK/APB limits of 400/200/100 MHz. Different source assumptions require a new reviewed profile.

Local UART arithmetic does not qualify a link¶

At requested 115200 baud, oversampling 16 and prescaler 1, the selected HSI64 kernel produces BRR556. Nominal baud is about 115107.914; the 62..65 MHz source envelope produces about 111510.791..116906.475 baud, or −3.202438..+1.481315% relative to the request. The lower endpoint already exceeds a hypothetical 2% local budget. Nominal divider accuracy alone cannot establish acceptable communication over the whole source envelope.

The platform checks local divider representability and exposes the selected source bounds. It does not invent a zero-error peer, require product peer-budget fields or silently choose another source. The external product must evaluate the complete transmitter, receiver, quantization, line and wakeup budget in RM0433 Rev9 section 48.5.8, then obtain physical evidence for its link.

Establish protection before speed and retain failed cleanup¶

The selected joint owner stores one four-byte effect record. It owns the reversible SYSCFG gate, voltage request, shared Flash timing, bus prescalers, PLL configuration/source and USART/I2C/ADC kernel selectors. It does not own consumer requests, buffers, worker tasks or a scheduler.

  1. Validate cold prerequisites, acquire the reversible gate and reject foreign overdrive. Prove VOS1 readiness and Flash LATENCY2/WRHIGHFREQ2 readback.

  2. Program the fixed bus dividers and wait 32 ordered RCC observations before programming PLL1. RM0433 section 8.7.8 allows HPRE propagation up to 16 slowest-APB periods; this profile has APB=HCLK/2. Readable HPRE alone is not proof that the effective divider has settled.

  3. Establish PLL1, observe readiness, request its source and prove actual SWS before publishing the nominal CPU clock. Acquire and verify the kernel selectors, then start System and selected consumers.

  4. Stop admission, drain and join consumers, and withdraw System. A failed consumer or System stop retains the clock owner and its protections.

  5. Restore only owned selectors; each may already be at its baseline or owned target. A foreign selector rejects without clobbering its register. Prove actual HSI selection before disabling PLL or reducing bus, Flash and voltage protections. Clear an effect only after its restoration is observed.

A startup error preserves its primary result while ordered cleanup reports its own failure and retained responsibility. A failed restore keeps the same owner alive for retry; timeout does not authorize releasing unproved effects. This is a single fixed cold profile, not a general-purpose clock tree manager.

High-profile Flash pulses require the observed V-cut, VOS1, HCLK, 2WS/WR2 and Cache-off combination. The high owner replaces the fixed-HSI optional Flash timing owner; both must not manage the same timing field. A pulse never retunes ACR. Original voltage, unswapped-bank, region, alignment and fresh-erase requirements still apply. All-FF readback does not prove virgin ECC cells. Busy-bank instruction fetch may stall; an unabortable pulse must settle before return, and failure to prove final withdrawal retains the calling frame through the existing terminal fault path. There is no tight physical deadline promise.

I2C timing requires explicit kernel and electrical bounds; PCLK is not its selected 64 MHz kernel. SPI divider selection uses 99.45 MHz, while its stored nominal source remains 96 MHz. For a 1.5 MHz ceiling it selects /128, rather than /64 from nominal arithmetic. Wire-tail drain and CS release remain required even after the caller’s deadline. ADC’s 16-bit storage does not establish measured effective resolution or calibrated voltage.

Selection cost, executed scope and next exits¶

Only the high profile emits the joint owner and selects clock.c. The four existing fixed-HSI/cache profiles retain their own lifecycle and do not call this owner. The four-byte record is a source-level selected-state fact, not a measured whole-image RAM delta; alignment, retained code and linked ELF costs must be observed separately. No allocation or extra hot-path forwarding layer is introduced by clock selection.
